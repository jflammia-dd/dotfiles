When writing planning, briefs or positioning docs, pull named customer and design-partner demand into the value/why sections wherever it is grounded in a source. Named demand strengthens the position far more than abstract capability framing.

**Why:** Concrete demand (a named design partner, a named customer, a prospect making something a contract blocker) is evidence leadership trusts. Generic "this is valuable" framing is not.

**How to apply:** Check the research/source docs for design-partner or customer demand before writing value sections. Cite the specific account and what they asked for. Never fabricate or overgeneralize demand: if a source has no documented demand (as CrowdStrike did in the UEBA Q3 work), say nothing rather than invent a signal. Verify each named demand traces to an internal source.
